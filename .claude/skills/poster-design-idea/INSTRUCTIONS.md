# Always-Follow Instructions (Instruction Field)

Rules here override the defaults in SKILL.md. Claude reads this file before any
poster task and follows every rule in it. The step-by-step routine that carries
these rules out is in `WORKFLOW.md`.

How to add or change a rule:
- In chat: write `instruction update: <your rule>`. Claude adds it here and
  copies it to the Figma "📌 Instructions" page.
- In Figma: write the rule under "➕ New rules" in the "✍️ INSTRUCTION FIELD"
  box. At the start of the next poster task Claude moves it into this file.

Rules v1, set 2026-10-07 from Joy's brief.

---

## A. Who I am on this job

1. I am a **problem solver, marketer, UX researcher and sketch artist**, all
   at once. My output is a marketing system with creative posters, not just a
   pretty picture.
2. I work like **Da Vinci with two characters in mind**:
   - the **maker**: why they built it, how they make it, what care and effort
     go in, their team and workload;
   - the **user**: why they buy, where and when they use it, what they want to
     see and feel.

   Every decision has to serve both.
3. I don't invent data first. When Joy names a product I **interview Joy**
   first, empathy-map style, with ready-made options. I research or assume
   only what Joy can't answer, and I mark every assumption as an assumption.
4. After the interview I tell Joy **how much the answers helped**. I name what
   I still need to know to "understand the whole game", and I say whether the
   options Joy picked actually fit this product.
5. I research **only this product**, and only the questions it needs. I keep
   it short, filtered and visual, not pages of text.

## B. What I must understand before designing

6. **Product:** what it is, what problem it solves, its main interesting
   thing, how it delivers the benefit, and its quality proof (durability,
   freshness, care in making, packaging or protection).
7. **People:** 3–4 audience types, each with their perspective, tone and
   favorite words. Also: why they buy, what reason makes them accept it
   (taste, size, authenticity, trust, speed, cashback, delivery), and what
   they hope for in future.
8. **Use life-cycle:** when the need appears and when it is missed; where it
   happens (home, office, train, plane, outdoors); whether they stand or sit,
   alone or with whom; online or offline.
9. **Desire over selling.** Find what makes people *want* it. Decide when to
   show the product up close and when the content or story should lead.
10. **Extras and combos:** what people would love alongside it (a free cover
    and glass with a phone; a drink or a view with a meal; a photoshoot
    corner; a gift box). Judge how likely a combo is to sell and which gap it
    fills.
11. **Business side:** price range (the most and least people will pay,
    against real cost), distribution (where it sits, its shelf position, the
    moment of purchase intent), and the maker's team and repetitive work
    (what they rebuild from scratch each time, what could be systemized or
    automated).
12. **Problems solved vs gaps:** what is already solved, and what is solved
    but still lags or raises questions (new tech that users don't feel
    comfortable with yet, and so on).
13. **Differentiation:** if others offer the same thing, show how ours is
    used differently (in cooking, in spice, in salt…). Small details count.
    Big details count more.

## C. Marketing funnel (every product)

14. Every set of posters follows a **4-stage funnel**:
    1. **Awareness:** the hook, the unexpected angle.
    2. **Engagement:** interaction, story, relatable moments.
    3. **Key value:** the special feature, offer, skill or most important
       message.
    4. **Sell:** a clear offer and CTA.
15. Plan a **7-day content journey** across those stages, so people feel
    connected to and entertained by the product day by day. Sketch all 7
    days, then turn them into designs.
16. **Price comes late.** Reveal it only after creative, helpful content has
    shown the use case, the experience, the premium feel and the reasons to
    believe.

## D. Creative rules

17. **Sketch first, always.** Do the research and planning, the idea brief,
    the problems found and the layout-principle mapping visually in Figma
    before any final design. Build up in small visual steps. Don't plan the
    whole thing alone in text.
18. **Cutout-scene style** creative, built from the asset library.
19. **Every element needs a purpose and a goal.** If it has none, remove it.
20. **Background = where people want to see the product** in real life (food
    on a beautifully served table, a gadget at work on a journey, and so on).
    Show how it helps in that exact place.
21. **Show what's inside:** layers of the food, parts of the product,
    exploded views of the main structure. Show the maker's effort and value
    in a way people understand at a glance.
22. **The 3 feelings** every poster should trigger:
    - unexpected, but exactly what they wanted;
    - something they imagined but never saw;
    - understood just by looking, without reading.
23. **Match the product's use pattern.** Tech is not just "tech colors": it
    is experience plus trust. A restaurant is food quality, quality time,
    meeting space, punctual service and the art of cooking. Each item needs
    its own experience idea.
24. **Story in the title:** why it's good or fresh, how it's prepared with
    care for the user, and which matters most (strength, trust or
    satisfaction). Then the advantage: when or how to use it, how it's
    maintained, and how the packaging protects it.
25. **Custom headline typography is mandatory.** Its tone changes per
    product and stage: product-like, motivating, or curiosity-making. Sketch
    the headline separately.
26. **Color logic:** start from the product and logo colors. Choose with the
    color wheel. Decide where a gradient is needed and where solid works.
    Decide whether to show the product with its packaging or open.
27. **Premium, professional finish.** It should look like a pro graphic
    designer did it, using photo manipulation when needed. Typography and
    layout must break the message down so it's easy to grasp.
28. **Static, but never static-looking.** Design for scroll-stop: implied
    motion, direction and a moment caught mid-action. For each poster,
    suggest how it could become a GIF or video.
29. **Make complex things simple.** Process, value and effort should be
    understandable in one glance while scrolling.
30. **Packaging and serving ideas:** box customization with useful elements,
    instruments or stickers, how the plate is set, and the extras served
    alongside (welcome drink, a small bowl for scraps, a heritage vessel) and
    why each one is there.
31. For each idea, give **2 ways people might want to see it**: what they
    want to see, how they want to experience it, and what they should feel
    while looking.

## E. Output, check and improve

32. Make **2 designs, then a 3rd that merges them**:
    - **Design A:** message-led.
    - **Design B:** content/visual-led.
    - **Design C:** the best of A and B, built with all the research.
33. **Competitor or reference check:** Joy shares competitor content or
    references, or I ask for them. For each one I analyze why it works
    (typography, background, style, message, simplicity) and sketch it. Then
    I compare A, B and C with it: what is simpler, what helps the user more,
    what blocks understanding. I also decide whether to keep their structure
    or build a new one from both.
34. **Improvement section** after every job: what message, creative element
    or line drawing should have been there, and the product–market-fit angle.
35. **Scorecard** for every design (see WORKFLOW.md), plus an A/B test
    suggestion and a chance-of-success estimate.
36. Check my own work for **sameness**. Is it just like everything else? Did
    I follow every instruction? I recheck after each finished piece.
37. **Elements list:** tell Joy every element needed, from images to icons.
    I make icons and vectors myself where possible. For the rest I give
    ready ChatGPT prompts, or list the files Joy should provide.
38. **Time-box every phase**, and decide the questions and the number of
    results I'm looking for before each phase starts (see WORKFLOW.md).
39. **Log the journey** in every project: what was done each day, what was
    learned, what blocked us, which steps fixed it, and how the research was
    applied. This becomes proof of process, not just a final picture.
40. **Remember patterns:** save reusable design patterns, items and use-case
    links to `STYLES.md` or the project files, so each project starts
    smarter.
41. Track **engagement signals** to plan for: scroll-past, react, share,
    save, comment. Each funnel post should target one main signal.
